The search

Short shared spacer, blown apart in some strains

Find divergent pairs with a ≤500 bp spacer in a reference strain, then look for the same pair pulled apart in other strains of that species. Insertion elements favour short AT-rich intergenic DNA — the same property that makes a spacer a shared promoter.

Result — yes, it generalizes

Two new functionally-coupled pairs, disrupted

metE / metR  ·  Vibrio cholerae
395 bp → 14.7 kb, inserted element carries an integrase. MetR is the documented transcriptional activator of metE — a regulator/target pair, exactly the torS/torT architecture.
yyaT / yybA  ·  Bacillus subtilis
119 bp → 1.5 kb by a clean IS4-family transposase — a different class of element. YybA's predicted regulon includes yyaT.
Positive control: the pipeline independently re-found torS/torT disrupted in 3 of 20 randomly-sampled E. coli strains.
But: of 26 disrupted pairs, most are not functionally coupled — including flk/pdxB, documented in the literature as sharing a divergent promoter purely by coincidence.

The catch — and the real lesson

Is there selection on this? Same data, three answers.

unvalidated coupling annotation validated annotation (AUC 0.87)

The keyword heuristic — "is a transcriptional regulator sitting next door?" — gave a nominally significant enrichment. It was an artifact: most of its hits fail literature checking (fabR/sthA, btsS/mlrA …). With a validated coupling score the signal vanishes. The bottleneck was annotation quality, not genome count.

Where it stands

The annotation layer is fixed. The sample isn't.

Coupling scores now come from STRING's non-positional channels only — its neighborhood channel is built from conserved genomic adjacency, so using it would reward our pairs for being adjacent and manufacture the result. Gene-symbol joins covered just 1–6% of pairs in three species; blastp orthologs lifted coverage to 89%.

Power analysis: a 5× effect would need only ~700 genomes and we don't see one, so strong selection either way is provisionally excluded. A 2× effect needs ~2,000 genomes — and 2–5× more after correcting for phylogeny, since our 108 strain-level hits collapse to just 26 independent events.

Where I'd want the group's input: better ground truth for functional coupling than STRING textmining (RegPrecise? RegulonDB?) — it under-calls in non-model species, which is exactly where our hits are. And: is the allele-frequency design (polymorphic vs fixed insertions) worth more than adding genomes?
